Frequently Asked Questions

Questions About Launching Lu-177 Therapy

What is needed to establish a Pluvicto program?

Key considerations include appropriate radioactive materials licensing, authorized physicians, radiation safety oversight, suitable facilities, trained staff, monitoring instruments, clinical workflows, and required documentation. Requirements vary by regulatory jurisdiction and institutional circumstances.

Can an existing nuclear medicine department provide Lu-177 therapy?

An existing department may provide a foundation, but its radioactive materials license, authorized personnel, equipment, facilities, and procedures must be evaluated for the proposed therapy activities.

Does a hospital always need a new radioactive materials license?

Not necessarily. Depending on the existing license and regulatory jurisdiction, an amendment or other authorization may be needed. The licensee and radiation safety officer should coordinate with the relevant regulator.

How can medical physicists support a theranostics program?

Depending on their qualifications and authorizations, medical physicists may contribute to radiation safety assessments, facility planning, instrumentation, quality assurance, imaging-related evaluations, staff education, and program readiness activities.

Do therapeutic and diagnostic ABR certifications replace nuclear medicine authorizations?

No. These are distinct professional specialties. Nuclear medical physics certification and radioactive materials authorizations have separate requirements where applicable. Each regulated activity must be assigned to appropriately qualified personnel.

Can you assist an existing Lu-177 program?

Yes. Projects may include quality assurance reviews, documentation improvements, radiation safety planning, operational assessments, and program expansion support, subject to the agreed scope of services.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions About Starting a Lu-177 Program

What does a hospital need before starting a Pluvicto program?

Requirements depend on the institution's regulatory jurisdiction and existing radioactive materials license. Important considerations include appropriately authorized physicians, licensed radioactive material activities, radiation safety procedures, suitable facilities, trained personnel, instrumentation, and operational workflows. A site-specific assessment is essential.

Can an existing nuclear medicine department add Lu-177 therapy?

Potentially. Existing nuclear medicine infrastructure may provide a useful foundation, but the institution must confirm that its license, authorized personnel, facilities, procedures, and radiation protection program cover the proposed therapy activities.

Is a radioactive materials license amendment required?

That depends on the scope and conditions of the existing license and the applicable regulatory authority. Institutions should coordinate with their radiation safety officer and regulator to determine whether an amendment or other authorization is necessary.

What can a medical physics consultant contribute?

Depending on qualifications and authorization, medical physics consultants can assist with radiation safety planning, facility assessments, exposure calculations, instrumentation, quality assurance, procedures, training, and program readiness evaluations.

Do you support existing programs as well as new programs?

Yes. Consulting projects may address initial planning, implementation readiness, operational reviews, documentation improvements, or ongoing physics support, subject to the agreed scope and applicable professional requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions About Launching Lu-177 Therapy

What is needed to establish a Pluvicto program?

Considerations include applicable radioactive materials licensing, authorized personnel, radiation safety oversight, suitable facilities, trained staff, instrumentation, clinical workflows, and required documentation.

Can an existing nuclear medicine department provide Lu-177 therapy?

Possibly. Existing license scope, authorized personnel, equipment, facilities, and procedures must be evaluated for the proposed activity.

Does a hospital always need a new radioactive materials license?

Not necessarily. Depending on the existing license and jurisdiction, an amendment or other authorization may be required. The licensee and RSO should coordinate with the regulator.

How can medical physicists support a theranostics program?

Depending on qualifications and authorizations, physicists may contribute to radiation safety, facility planning, instrumentation, QA, imaging, education, and readiness activities.

Do therapeutic and diagnostic ABR certifications replace nuclear medicine authorizations?

No. These are distinct professional qualifications. Each regulated activity must be assigned to appropriately qualified and authorized personnel.

Can you assist an existing Lu-177 program?

Projects may include QA reviews, documentation improvements, radiation safety planning, operational assessments, and expansion support, subject to an agreed scope.

Does the Lu-177 calculator estimate patient dose?

No. It models physical radioactive decay only. Patient-specific dosimetry requires additional validated clinical information and qualified review.
